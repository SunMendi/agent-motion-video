## Remaining priorities

### 1. The hero is still often a card, not the subject

At 0–10.4s the actual identities are small emoji badges inside large white rectangles. At 10.4–19.3s the model name sits in another console above a small route panel. At 25.3–33.1s the route and email are still two persistent interface panels. At 33.1–43.1s tall cards reserve blank interiors until the second identity arrives.

The reference uses recognizable product artwork and selective labels to carry the meaning. Increasing the panel width does not increase the subject's importance or visual specificity. Many 15–24 px source labels remain only 5–8 px in the phone preview.

Repair the first 43 seconds around a small cast of independently animated subjects. Use a large identity/portrait mark, message/envelope, patch fragment, relay, or recipient. Remove outer shells when they do not explain a real interface. Enlarge the useful illustration, not its surrounding blank card. Keep only essential body type.

### 2. Headings and summaries keep creating a second narration track

The chapter pills have largely been removed, which helps. Full-sentence headings still restate the current narration, and many scenes end in persistent microtext summary bars. The code scene, for example, has a heading, code window, PR-status card, and result footer. The footer adds another reading level without adding a visible operation.

Reduce the display text to a short identifier, required figure, distinction, or outcome. Let the picture do the explanatory work. Preserve the useful top captions and the resolved semantic event timing.

### 3. Setup and motion are still too repetitive

The model introduction mainly cycles a probe along a short line until blocked. The Tor section retains an entire email draft while a dot cycles through the route. The second-identity scene uses an audit scanner and progress bar during much of the setup. The cache section improves its architecture but still relies on cycling cell highlights before the main event.

These loops are rendered activity, not additional story development. Use staged framing and meaningful subject changes when the current voice introduces new information. After the point is established, a deliberate readable hold is valid. Do not keep adding loops solely to stay below a three-second pixel-motion threshold.

### 4. Some important emphasis remains on independent local timing

The main storyboard event names are fixed. Smaller narrative stages still use hardcoded frame thresholds:

- The cache network changes to severed at local frame 65, about 69.93s. The reviewed phrase explicitly saying there is no network starts at 75.22s. Decide whether this is establishing an initial isolated condition or demonstrating a disconnection; the current cut animation anticipates that explanation by about 5.3 seconds.
- The second closing perspective gains focus at local frame 100, about 92.70s. The first question continues until 93.68s; the alternative begins then, and its warning phrase starts at 94.58s. Move the emphasis to the actual rhetorical turn rather than a three-second timer.

Bind these meaningful changes to reviewed phrase IDs or add matching compiled events. Keep local timing for purely decorative secondary movement, not the logic of the argument.

### 5. Sequence continuity and visual finish remain underdeveloped

Scene boundaries are still direct cuts between similar cream-background layouts. Subjects seldom transform or travel into the following beat. Generic emoji, thin UI borders, glow badges, and small status labels form much of the asset language. The reference's coherent subject artwork, selective type, changing composition, and section transitions are more fully developed.

Choose one illustration treatment and a restrained transition vocabulary. Carry the message into the relay sequence, or the disputed patch into reviewer discovery. Use real identity assets or deliberately authored type where appropriate. Demonstrate a representative transition and detailed asset at phone size before extending those choices.
