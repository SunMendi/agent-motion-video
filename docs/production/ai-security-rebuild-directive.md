## The visual idea

Make the viewer see a sequence of trust boundaries being crossed: an identity is misrepresented, a contribution enters a review process, a second identity appears to support the first, and isolated systems communicate through an unexpected shared resource. Present these as narrated claims and explanatory reconstructions, with source uncertainty recorded. A convincing diagram does not verify a claim.

Choose an editorial technology documentary treatment. Use genuine publisher/product identity assets and selective source-page crops for recognition. Use authored, layered object animation for the mechanisms. Keep the screen focused on one important relationship at a time.

Use warm paper `#F3F0E8` and near-black `#151515` as the main field and ink. Use muted orange `#E75B36` to draw attention to an intrusion or disputed element. Use teal `#167F97` for a route or isolation boundary. Preserve authentic brand colors inside identity assets. Check contrast in the actual render. The palette is a direction for this rebuild, not a replacement global theme.

Use solid shapes, crisp masks, readable crops, and deliberate spatial movement. Keep shadows modest. The continuous visual thread should be the same contribution, account, packet, or boundary being changed across related shots. Avoid decorative cyber grids, neon incident pills, persistent dashboards, and unrelated particles.

Captions stay at the top with the approved Bengali font and shaping. Use the shared 1080×1920 layout and `SceneStage` inside each sequence. Plan the visible artwork across the action stage rather than compressing an entire desktop UI into its middle. Start essential labels around 48–64 source pixels and judge them in the 360×640 preview; these are starting sizes, not global tokens. Crop into important interface details. Avoid adding a body headline to summarize what the captions already say.

## Design acceptance

Every important subject should be identifiable without reading a paragraph. A model introduction needs recognizable identity, not its name hidden in a tiny incident badge. A mechanism needs a visible state change, not an animated headline.

Inspect the worst-looking frame in every shot, including its entrance and exit. Check that the subject is large enough, captions remain separate, the composition uses the vertical stage deliberately, and necessary interface details remain legible. Check actual artwork bounds; a full-size transparent wrapper does not fill the composition.

For each important voice phrase, point to what changes on screen at that moment. If the answer is only “a caption changes” or “the whole card slowly zooms,” redesign the action. A deliberate reading hold is valid when recorded as such. Do not add meaningless movement to satisfy a metric.

Use primary sources for displayed names, captured screens, figures, and quotes. Keep illustrative labels visible where a reconstruction could be mistaken for evidence. Preserve the original voice; request permission before changing supplied narration or instructions.

The design lead's review inputs are the worker's beat map, three style frames, hardest animated sample, and final render evidence with findings. These are inspectable artifacts produced during autonomous work, not mandatory permission checkpoints. Completion means a coherent explanatory sequence that survives phone-size viewing, together with honest production and review records.
